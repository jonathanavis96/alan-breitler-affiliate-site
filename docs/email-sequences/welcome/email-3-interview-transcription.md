If you do qualitative research, this is the email in the sequence most likely to give back actual hours of your life.

The accepted rule of thumb for manual transcription is four to six hours per hour of audio. For a doctoral student running 30 interviews for a dissertation, that is a full month of work before the analysis even starts. Until recently, the only honest alternatives were either paying $90+ per interview for human transcription (Rev.com and similar services) or living with the time cost.

That changed when Otter.ai became good enough.

**The honest version of how good it actually is**

On clean audio with clear speakers, Otter produces transcripts at around 90 to 95% word accuracy. That is good enough to scan, code, and locate quotes. It is not good enough to publish a verbatim quote without checking. The workflow that works is:

1. Record your interview as you normally would.
2. Upload to Otter (or let it capture live via Zoom/Meet/Teams). Wait 3-5 minutes.
3. Skim the transcript at 1.5x audio playback, correcting errors as you go.
4. For a 60-minute interview, this takes about 25-35 minutes — roughly 4-6× faster than manual transcription from scratch.

What makes step 3 actually work is that the transcript is synchronised with the audio. Click any word and it jumps to that moment in the recording. We use this constantly during correction and later during coding — being able to click on a quote to verify it is the feature that turns Otter from "approximate text" into "research-usable transcript."

**Where it fails**

Three failure modes you need to plan around:

- **Strong non-native accents.** Word accuracy drops to 75-85% for some accent profiles. Plan for higher correction time.
- **Disciplinary jargon.** Biomedical terminology, legal Latin, chemistry nomenclature — routinely misheard. We watched Otter transcribe "operationalisation" as "operational session" in a psychology interview. A small error, but it would have been embarrassing in a published quote.
- **Overlapping speech and focus groups.** Speaker identification confuses itself when three or more voices speak in quick succession. For focus group work, Otter saves time but does not eliminate the listening-back step.

**One more thing — research ethics**

Otter is a third-party participant in your meetings. Your interview audio is processed on Otter's servers. Most universities permit this for low-sensitivity work, but research involving human subjects may require ethics-board approval before introducing a third-party transcription service. Check before you start.

For high-stakes work where every word must be exact (legal research, oral history projects, anything quoting vulnerable participants), Rev's human transcription at ~$1.50/minute remains the safer choice. For routine research interviews, Otter wins on time and cost.
